Speaker 1 • Physical Characterization

Harmonic Vibration vs. Transient Shock

Why Classical Static Assumptions Fail Catastrophically

1. Continuous Steady-State Vibration

Governed by infinite harmonic forcing $F(t) = F_0 \sin(\omega t)$. Transient components decay; failure is governed by high-cycle fatigue over $10^5 - 10^7$ cycles.

2. Transient Shock Disturbance

Characterized by short duration $t_0 \lesssim \tau_n = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}$. The disturbance terminates before multiple cycles complete. Failure is dictated by instantaneous peak stress rupture.

The Fundamental Static Fallacy
$$\delta_{\text{static}} = \frac{F_0}{k} \quad \ll \quad x_{\max} = \text{DLF} \cdot \frac{F_0}{k}$$

Because the mass possesses inertia, dynamic overshoot creates a Dynamic Load Factor (DLF) up to $2.0$.

Harmonic: $t \to \infty$
Shock Pulse: $t_0 \lesssim \tau_n$

Speaker 2 • Mathematical Formulation

SDOF Dynamic Equations & Transfer Function

Laplace Transform & Generalized Dynamic Impedance

1. Time-Domain Canonical Equation
$$m \ddot{x}(t) + c \dot{x}(t) + k x(t) = F(t) \implies \ddot{x} + 2\zeta\omega_n \dot{x} + \omega_n^2 x = \frac{F(t)}{m}$$
2. Laplace Domain Transformation
$$(m s^2 + c s + k) X(s) - m s x(0) - m \dot{x}(0) - c x(0) = F(s)$$
3. System Transfer Function $H(s)$ & Impedance $Z(s)$
$$H(s) = \frac{X(s)}{F(s)} = \frac{1}{m(s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_n s + \omega_n^2)}, \quad Z(s) = \frac{1}{H(s)} = m s^2 + c s + k$$

Speaker 2 • Convolution Mechanics

Impulse Response & Duhamel Integral

Superposition of Infinitesimal Impulses $F(\tau) d\tau$

1. Unit Impulse Response Function $g(t)$
$$g(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{H(s)\} = \frac{1}{m\omega_d} e^{-\zeta\omega_n t} \sin(\omega_d t) \cdot u(t)$$ $$\text{For } \zeta = 0: \quad g(t) = \frac{1}{m\omega_n} \sin(\omega_n t)$$
2. Duhamel (Convolution) Integral
$$x(t) = \int_0^t F(\tau) g(t - \tau) d\tau = \frac{1}{m\omega_n} \int_0^t F(\tau) \sin\omega_n(t - \tau) \, d\tau$$
3. SDOF Under Base Acceleration Shock $\ddot{y}(t)$
$$z(t) = x(t) - y(t) = -\frac{1}{\omega_n} \int_0^t \ddot{y}(\tau) \sin\omega_n(t - \tau) \, d\tau$$

Speaker 2 • Time-Domain Partitioning

The Two Temporal Eras

Forced Phase ($t \le t_0$) vs. Residual Free Ringdown ($t > t_0$)

Era 1: Forced Phase ($0 \le t \le t_0$)

Shock force is actively pumping energy into the system:

$$x_1(t) = \frac{1}{m\omega_n} \int_0^t F(\tau) \sin\omega_n(t - \tau) \, d\tau$$ Yields Primary SRS: $\max_{0 \le t \le t_0} |x(t)|$

Era 2: Residual Phase ($t > t_0$)

External force vanishes ($F(\tau) = 0$ for $\tau > t_0$); integral freezes at $t_0$:

$$x_2(t) = \frac{1}{m\omega_n} \int_0^{t_0} F(\tau) \sin\omega_n(t - \tau) \, d\tau$$ Yields Residual SRS: $\max_{t > t_0} |x(t)|$
Boundary Matching at $t = t_0$: $x_1(t_0) = x_2(t_0)$ and $\dot{x}_1(t_0) = \dot{x}_2(t_0)$ determine pure free vibration ringdown.

Speaker 2 • Canonical Derivation 1

Rectangular Shock Pulse Response

Dynamic Doubling ($\text{DLF}=2.0$) & Residual Cancellation

Era 1 ($0 \le t \le t_0$): Step Excitation
$$\frac{x(t)}{\delta_{\text{st}}} = 1 - \cos\omega_n t \implies \text{DLF}_{\text{primary}} = \begin{cases} 1 - \cos\left(\frac{2\pi t_0}{\tau_n}\right), & t_0/\tau_n < 0.5 \\ 2.0, & t_0/\tau_n \ge 0.5 \end{cases}$$
Era 2 ($t > t_0$): Residual Free Vibration
$$\frac{x(t)}{\delta_{\text{st}}} = \cos\omega_n(t - t_0) - \cos\omega_n t \implies \mathbf{\text{DLF}_{\text{residual}} = 2 \left| \sin\left(\frac{\pi t_0}{\tau_n}\right) \right|}$$

The Residual Cancellation Phenomenon

At integer ratios $t_0/\tau_n = 1, 2, 3\dots$, $\sin(\pi t_0/\tau_n) = 0$. Residual vibration is identically zero! The system completes full cycles and halts dead at $t_0$.

Peak DLF = 2.0
Zero Residual Node ($t_0 = \tau_n$)

Speaker 2 • Canonical Derivation 2

Half-Sine Shock Pulse Response

Analytical Formulation for Ground Impact Pulse

Era 1 ($0 \le t \le t_0$): Forced Wave Interaction
$$\frac{x(t)}{\delta_{\text{st}}} = \frac{1}{1 - (\frac{\tau_n}{2t_0})^2} \left[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{t_0}\right) - \left(\frac{\tau_n}{2t_0}\right) \sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau_n}\right) \right]$$
Primary SRS Peak Maximization
$$\text{DLF}_{\text{primary}} = \left| \frac{1}{1 - \frac{\tau_n}{2t_0}} \sin\left( \frac{2\pi \frac{\tau_n}{2t_0}}{1 + \frac{\tau_n}{2t_0}} \right) \right| \quad \Longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Peak DLF} \approx 1.76 \text{ at } \frac{t_0}{\tau_n} \approx 0.80}$$
Era 2 ($t > t_0$): Residual SRS Formulation
$$\text{DLF}_{\text{residual}} = \left| \frac{2 \cos\left(\frac{\pi t_0}{\tau_n}\right)}{1 - \left(\frac{2t_0}{\tau_n}\right)^2} \right| \quad (\text{Zeros at } t_0/\tau_n = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5\dots)$$
Peak DLF = 1.76 ($t_0/\tau_n = 0.8$)

Speaker 2 • Asymptotic Insights & Hand-Off

Asymptotic Master Insights

Impulsive Limit vs. Quasi-Static Limit & Hand-Off

Impulsive Limit ($t_0 \ll \tau_n$)

Pulse finishes before mass moves. Response depends only on net impulse $\mathcal{I} = \int F dt$:

$$\text{DLF} = 2\pi \left(\frac{\mathcal{I}}{F_0 t_0}\right) \left(\frac{t_0}{\tau_n}\right)$$ Waveform shape is irrelevant!

Quasi-Static Limit ($t_0 \gg \tau_n$)

Force changes so slowly that inertia forces vanish:

$$\text{DLF}_{\text{primary}} \to 1.0, \quad \text{DLF}_{\text{residual}} \to 0$$ Pure static equilibrium
